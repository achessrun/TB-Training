TB-1 Prep NEW Rev —

A trial balance is not organized the way financial statements are. It is organized the way the audit is. Once you see that, the groupings that look wrong start looking deliberate.

Accounts receivable is an asset, yet it is filed under Revenue. Accounts payable is a liability, yet it is filed under Expense. Neither is a mistake. This trial balance groups accounts by audit area rather than by financial statement caption, and accounts land together when one set of procedures gives evidence about all of them.

Each area rolls up to a numbered lead schedule. That number is the index reference you write in the corner of every workpaper for that area, and it is how a reviewer finds your work six months later.

There are no balances on this page on purpose. The question here is why an account belongs where it does, and what it puts at risk — not what it happens to be worth this year. The full trial balance is one click away whenever you need the figures, and every account below shows its workbook row.
