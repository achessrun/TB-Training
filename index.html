TB-1 Prep NEW Rev —

A trial balance is not organized the way financial statements are. It is organized the way the audit is. Once you see that, the groupings that look wrong start looking deliberate.

The obvious question on your first engagement is why Accounts Receivable, an asset, is filed under Revenue, and why Accounts Payable, a liability, is filed under Expense. The answer is that this trial balance is grouped by audit area, not by financial statement caption. Accounts land together when the same procedures give evidence about all of them at once.

Each group rolls up to a numbered lead schedule. That number is the index reference you write in the corner of every workpaper you prepare for that area, and it is how a reviewer finds your work six months later. One group below still has no reference assigned, which is itself something to raise.

Every account carries a note explaining what it is and what to do with it. The red tickmarks in the margin flag the ones that teach something beyond their own balance.

Tickmark legend

Auditors annotate in the margin rather than rewriting the schedule. These four marks appear throughout the accounts below, and they carry the parts worth memorizing.
